Why the city is pink

Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II founded Jaipur in 1727 and planned it on a grid, which was unusual for its time. In 1876 the walled city was painted pink to welcome the Prince of Wales, and the colour stayed.

Today the old city still follows its original plan, with wide bazaars, gates in every direction and the hill forts looking down on it. Drag the slider to repaint it.

    Where Jaipur eats

    Old sweet shops, kachori counters and chai stalls that locals queue at. Most are inside or near the walled city and are easy to string together on foot or by auto.

    Pyaaz kachoriFried pastry stuffed with spiced onion. The city's signature snack.
    GhewarA honeycomb-shaped sweet soaked in syrup, often topped with rabri.
    Dal baati churmaBaked wheat balls with lentils and a sweet crumbled churma.
    Mirchi vadaA large chilli in a gram-flour batter, fried and served hot.
    Lassi in a kulhadThick, sweet, with malai on top, served in a clay cup.
    Laal maasA fiery red mutton curry. Vegetarians can skip it, there is plenty else.

    A one-day food trail

    A sample order that moves from breakfast to late evening without much backtracking. Swap stops freely.

    1. MorningKachori and a sweet at Rawat Mishthan Bhandar, then a lassi on MI Road.
    2. MiddayA Rajasthani thali or dal kachori at LMB in Johari Bazaar.
    3. Late afternoonMasala chai and a bun samosa at Gulab Ji Chai.
    4. EveningKesar kulfi near Hawa Mahal, then walk the bazaar.
    5. NightStreet food in Ram Niwas Bagh at Masala Chowk. Check its hours first.

    Compiled from public food guides, including Outlook Traveller, LBB, Time Out and Holidify. Menus, prices and opening hours change, so confirm locally. Many places are cash only.

    Before you go

    October to MarchThe best time to visit. Days are pleasant, evenings are cool, and it is the easiest season for outdoor sightseeing and street food.
    April to JuneVery hot. Plan forts for early morning and rest in the afternoon.
    July to SeptemberMonsoon. The hills turn green and crowds thin, but expect humidity and rain.

    Getting there

    • By air Jaipur International Airport, in Sanganer, south of the city.
    • By train Jaipur Junction is the main station, well connected to Delhi, Mumbai and Ahmedabad.
    • By road Roughly 5 to 6 hours from Delhi.

    Getting around

    • App cabs The easiest way to avoid haggling.
    • Autos and e-rickshaws Agree the fare before you get in, or insist on the meter.
    • Full-day cab Worth it for Amer, Jaigarh and Nahargarh together.

    Stay smart

    • Buy tickets at the official counters or official sites, and ask about combined tickets.
    • If someone says a fort is closed today, check at the gate yourself.
    • Compare two shops before buying gems or carpets. Guides often take commissions.
    • Cover shoulders and knees at temples, and remove footwear where asked.

    What to buy

    • Block-printed fabric Sanganer and Bapu Bazaar.
    • Jewellery and gems Johari Bazaar. Buy from established shops.
    • Mojari shoes Bapu Bazaar and Nehru Bazaar.
    • Blue pottery Jaipur's glazed ceramics, best bought from a studio.
